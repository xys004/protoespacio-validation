\section{Introduction}
\label{p:intro}

The $(3{+}1)$-dimensional Dirac equation, the Clifford algebra it sits on,
and the spinorial Lorentz generators that organize it, all live
comfortably in the continuum: they assume a smooth manifold and a
continuous symmetry group. Whether the same algebraic edifice can be
obtained as an effective infrared sector of a discrete underlying
dynamics is a long-standing question, going back to Wilson's lattice
fermions~\cite{Wilson1974} and the Nielsen--Ninomiya
theorem~\cite{NielsenNinomiya1981a,NielsenNinomiya1981b}, and revisited
in the past decade in the quantum-walk (QW) and quantum-cellular-automata
frameworks~\cite{Arrighi2018,Farrelly2020,FarrellyShort2014,BisioDAriano2015}.
The answer is, by now, clearly \enquote{yes} at the level of low-energy
effective Hamiltonians. What has not been systematized is the
\emph{verification} side: each derivation in the literature is by hand,
each algebraic identity is reproved in each paper, and the link between
a given continuum result and the discrete model that produces it lives
in the head of the author.

This paper makes three coordinated contributions, all anchored on the
single ladder of Fig.~\ref{p:fig:ladder}. The first is the
\emph{ladder itself}: we assemble SSH in $1{+}1$, the tight-binding
model of graphene~\cite{SuSchriefferHeeger1979,Semenoff1984,CastroNeto2009},
a split-step honeycomb QW, and three-dimensional Weyl/Dirac
semimetals~\cite{Burkov2018} into one coherent
construction with uniform notation and a single set of internal degrees
of freedom $(\sigma_{i},\tau_{i})$. Throughout, we distinguish carefully
(i)~exact symmetries of the discrete step from (ii)~approximate
symmetries of the linearized infrared Hamiltonian, from (iii)~the
covariant Lorentz/Clifford organization of the continuum limit.

The second contribution is methodological. Every algebraic identity that
we claim as a result of the paper is paired with a machine-checked
symbolic test, written in either \texttt{sympy}~\cite{Meurer2017} (for
closed-form matrix identities, Taylor expansions, and commutators) or
\texttt{z3}~\cite{deMoura2008} (for combinatorial and order-theoretic
claims such as Nielsen--Ninomiya balance and the bipartite chirality
index). We choose this layer deliberately. Interactive proof assistants
such as Lean, Coq, or Isabelle~\cite{Buzzard2022} would re-encode the
whole construction into a typed proof; \texttt{sympy} and \texttt{z3}
instead certify the algebraic and combinatorial \emph{skeleton} on top of
which the physicist's manipulations rest, while integrating with the
working scientist's toolchain. Across the long-form companion of this
work, the policy adds up to $146$~tests across $25$ validator modules
that run end-to-end in under twenty seconds; the symbolic backbone of
\emph{this paper} is a subset of those tests, and we make the link
explicit at each section by inline references of the form
\texttt{\% verified-by: validators/\textless module\textgreater.py::\textless test\textgreater}.

The third contribution is to leave two well-posed open problems with a
first body of verified machinery already in place. The
\emph{spectral} branch concerns a global classification of the discrete
quasi-energy spectrum on the Brillouin torus, beyond the local infrared
analysis of band-crossing points. The \emph{structural} branch concerns
the formulation of the underlying \emph{protospace} on finite graphs not
assumed to be periodic, in the spirit of causal-set
approaches~\cite{BombelliLeeMeyerSorkin1987,Sorkin2003} but with the
algebraic content of the relativistic limit kept under explicit
verification. Both branches have already produced concrete verified
identities (see Sec.~\ref{p:open-problems}), which sets the stage for a
companion paper on the structural side.

\paragraph{Scope.} We do not claim that the ladder is unique, nor that
the protospace is fixed by it. We claim that the ladder is internally
consistent, that the relativistic algebra emerges as an effective
infrared \emph{organization} rather than as a microscopic symmetry, and
that the verification methodology is fine-grained enough to be useful
beyond this corpus. We use \enquote{the long form} of this work to
refer to the public companion repository where the validator suite, the
full $32$-chapter pedagogical manuscript, and the data behind the
$146$~tests are maintained; see Sec.~\ref{p:conclusions}.
