\section{Discussion and outlook}
\label{p:conclusions}

Two observations seem worth recording. The first is methodological: in
practice, the verification-as-build pipeline has turned out to be a
useful editorial discipline, not merely a correctness check. Writing a
section in the long form alongside its validator pulls one toward
stating each claim in a way that is unambiguous enough to test --- which
is exactly the form a reader needs anyway. The catches reported in
Sec.~\ref{p:verification} (a wrong sign in a covariance identity, an
inverted Hermiticity statement in a docstring, an overconfident
combinatorial predicate) are typical of the kind of slip that survives
hand-checking but does not survive a parameterized symbolic test. We
suspect the same discipline could productively be applied to other
discrete-to-continuum derivations beyond the present corpus. A first
instance already exists: since the first archived release, the suite
has grown to cover an induced-gravity layer --- position-dependent
tetrads read off from graded steps, torsion-free spin connections
solved on general 2D and 4D FRW tetrads, the signed Lichnerowicz
coefficient, and the proper-time integrals behind the cutoff scalings
of the induced Newton and cosmological terms --- under the same
claim-level policy; that layer will be presented separately.

The second observation is interpretive. The relativistic algebra ---
Clifford, $\Sigma^{\mu\nu}$, $SL(2,\bbC)$, a Lorentzian light cone, a
chirality balance --- has come out as the \emph{infrared organization}
of a family of discrete models, not as a microscopic symmetry. This
distinction is more than terminological: it predicts what the structural
branch (Sec.~\ref{p:open-problems}) has to look like. If the right
$\Proto$ is a finite graph rather than a torus, the algebraic content of
relativity should still be recoverable, but its rigid origin in
homotopy on $T^{d}$ has to be replaced by something else --- the
bipartite index bound is one early candidate, and a companion paper
devoted entirely to the structural branch is in preparation.
