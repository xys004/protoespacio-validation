\section{Verification methodology}
\label{p:verification}

The verification policy underlying the whole paper is simple to state
and tight in practice: \emph{the build of the PDF is gated by a test
suite}. Concretely:

\paragraph{Two backends, one policy.}
Algebraic identities are checked by \texttt{sympy}, which is appropriate
when the claim is an equality between matrix expressions, an expansion
to first or second order, or a manipulation of Clifford or Pauli
algebra. Combinatorial and order-theoretic claims (Nielsen--Ninomiya
balance, isotropy as SMT, bipartite index, structural consistency of
small causal sets) are checked by the SMT solver \texttt{z3}~\cite{deMoura2008},
which is appropriate when the natural domain is propositional or
arithmetic over small finite types.

\paragraph{Granularity.}
Each chapter of the long form (and each section of this paper, where
applicable) carries one or more comments of the form
\begin{center}\small\texttt{\% verified-by: validators/\textless module\textgreater.py::\textless test\textgreater}\end{center}
inserted immediately after the section heading. The total bookkeeping
amounts to $153$ such lines distributed across the 32 chapters of the
long form, covering all explicitly stated algebraic and combinatorial
claims.

\paragraph{Pipeline.}
A single script \texttt{scripts/build\_book.ps1} (with a parallel
\texttt{build\_book.sh} for a WSL/Linux build) runs the test suite
through \texttt{pytest} and proceeds with \texttt{latexmk} only on a
clean exit. With the current $146$ tests the full pipeline runs in under
twenty seconds. Of particular note: the policy caught three classes of
errors during development. (i) An overconfident formulation of a
chirality predicate based on the wrong combinatorial restriction, which
the SMT failed correctly. (ii) A wrong sign in the spinorial covariance
identity Eq.~\eqref{p:eq:cov-id}, caught by the closed-form symbolic
test. (iii) An inversion of the Hermitian / anti-Hermitian status of
rotational vs.\ boost generators in our docstrings, detected by
\texttt{::test\_rotation\_generators\_hermitian} and
\texttt{::test\_boost\_generators\_antihermitian}. In each case the
correction was algorithmic, not interpretive.

\paragraph{Cost / benefit.}
The cost of writing a verification module per chapter is approximately
two to four hours of careful work per claim; the cost of running the
full suite is negligible. The benefit is more important: the manuscript
becomes \emph{auditable}. A reader who wants to confirm any specific
algebraic identity of the paper can do so in under a minute by reading
the corresponding test, without needing to rerun the derivation by hand.

\paragraph{Comparison with related practices.}
There is a small body of recent work on machine-checked theoretical
physics, mostly using interactive theorem provers (Lean, Coq, Isabelle)
applied to relatively small theorems. The present approach is
deliberately coarser: \texttt{sympy} and \texttt{z3} suffice for the
algebraic and combinatorial layer we want to certify, do not require
re-encoding the entire physics in a typed proof assistant, and integrate
naturally with the working scientist's existing toolchain. The
trade-off is that we certify the algebra but not, for instance, the
infinite-dimensional limit of the continuum: that step remains a
mathematical theorem to be argued by hand. We see this as the right
granularity for the kind of pedagogical and verificational work
contained here.
