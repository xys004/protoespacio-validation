\section{Emergence criteria: causal cone, metric structure, chirality balance}
\label{p:criteria}

A consistent ladder is not enough on its own: one must also state what
counts as the relativistic limit being \emph{well posed}. We collect two
operational criteria, each verified by an explicit test.

\subsection{Causal and metric structure}
Given the Dirac-like dispersion $E(\vp)^{2} = v^{2}\lvert\vp\rvert^{2}+m^{2}$
that recurs throughout Sections~\ref{p:ssh}--\ref{p:weyl-dirac-3d}, the
group velocity obeys
\begin{equation}
v_{g}^{2}(\vp) \,-\, v^{2}
   \;=\; -\,\frac{m^{2}\,v^{2}}{v^{2}\lvert\vp\rvert^{2}+m^{2}} \;\le\; 0 ,
\label{p:eq:vg-bound}
\end{equation}
with saturation at $m=0$. This is the algebraic basis of the effective
light cone. The associated effective metric
$g^{\mu\nu}_{\mathrm{eff}} = \mathrm{diag}(-1,1/v^{2},1/v^{2},1/v^{2})$
has Lorentzian signature $(-,+,+,+)$ and determinant $-1/v^{6}$
(\texttt{validators/causality.py}). The discrete-time analogue obeys
$\cos(\varepsilon\,\Delta t) = 1-(v^{2}\vk^{2}+m^{2})\,\Delta t^{2}/2 + O(\Delta t^{3})$,
recovering the continuous dispersion at small step
(\texttt{validators/causalidad\_continuo\_vs\_discreto.py}).

The same algebraic structure tightens an emergent metric reading. For
an anisotropic effective Hamiltonian
$\Hop_{\mathrm{eff}} = \sum_{a} v_{a}\,\sigma_{a}\,p_{a}$, the squared
dispersion
$\Hop_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}=\sum_{a}v_{a}^{2}\,p_{a}^{2}\,\bbI$ reads as an
effective Riemannian metric
$g^{ab}_{\mathrm{eff}} = \mathrm{diag}(v_{x}^{2},v_{y}^{2},v_{z}^{2})$;
isotropy --- a single effective light cone --- is the condition
$v_{x}=v_{y}=v_{z}$. We encode this as an SMT problem and check that
isotropic solutions are realized and that anisotropy and isotropy
cannot coexist (\texttt{validators/isotropy.py}). For
position-dependent velocities $v_{a}(x)$ (variable tetrad), the same
calculation goes through pointwise
(\texttt{validators/triada.py}), which is the natural starting point
for any geometric continuation of the construction.

\subsection{Chirality balance: Nielsen--Ninomiya, with and without a torus}
On any compact periodic lattice the Nielsen--Ninomiya
theorem~\cite{NielsenNinomiya1981a,NielsenNinomiya1981b} forces the
total chirality summed over band nodes to vanish. Translated to a
combinatorial SMT statement: for $n$ even there exists an assignment of
charges $\chi_{i}\in\{\pm 1\}$ with $\sum_{i}\chi_{i}=0$; for $n$ odd no
such assignment exists, and no uniform-chirality configuration is ever
allowed (\texttt{validators/nielsen\_ninomiya.py}). On the eight-corner
family of the cubic Brillouin zone the same machinery handles the
$4{+}4$ distribution between the $\omega=0$ and $\omega=\pi$ sectors
of the discrete quasi-energy
(\texttt{validators/brillouin\_global.py}).

A strictly stronger statement holds \emph{without} invoking periodicity.
For a finite bipartite graph $G=(A\sqcup B,E)$, define
$\Gamma=\mathrm{diag}(+\bbI_{A},-\bbI_{B})$. Then
$\{\Gamma, A\}=0$, the spectrum of the adjacency operator is symmetric
in $E\mapsto -E$, and the nullity is bounded below by
$\lvert\lvert A\rvert-\lvert B\rvert\rvert$; on a maximum matching the
bound saturates. This is the discrete index theorem in the absence of a
reciprocal-space description (\texttt{validators/rama\_estructural.py}),
and we will use it in Sec.~\ref{p:open-problems} as the algebraic core
of the structural branch.
