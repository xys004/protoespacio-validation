\begin{letter}{Editorial Office \\ Quantum --- the open journal for quantum science}

\opening{Dear Editors,}

I am pleased to submit our manuscript ``Emergent Dirac dynamics in
$(3{+}1)$ dimensions from discrete quantum walks: a formally verified
pedagogical ladder'' for consideration by Quantum.

The paper assembles a single coherent ladder
SSH~$\to$~graphene~$\to$~honeycomb quantum walk~$\to$~Weyl/Dirac in
$(3{+}1)$, identifying the algebraic structures that survive as the
infrared organization of the discrete dynamics: the Clifford algebra of
$\gamma^{\mu}$, the spinorial Lorentz generators $\Sigma^{\mu\nu}$, the
chirality balance underlying Nielsen--Ninomiya, and a Lorentzian
effective light cone. Where the paper differs from earlier work in this
line --- most notably Arrighi \emph{et al.}\ (Phys.\ Rev.\ A 2018) and
Farrelly (Quantum 2020) --- is that the algebraic claims are paired one
by one with machine-checked symbolic tests (\texttt{sympy} for
closed-form identities, \texttt{z3} for combinatorial and
order-theoretic claims), and the manuscript build is gated by the test
suite. The methodology section makes this case explicitly and discusses
what the approach buys relative to interactive theorem provers.

We believe Quantum is the natural venue for the work: it sits in the
same line as Farrelly's review of quantum cellular automata (Quantum
2020) and addresses an audience that bridges quantum information, lattice
fermions, and emergent spacetime. The verification methodology is, we
believe, the most reusable contribution and is consistent with Quantum's
emphasis on reproducible and open science.

The paper is approximately 20 pages, single-column, with one bibliography
of four primary references plus the cited literature. All code is open
and reproduces every claim of the paper, including its build.

We have not submitted this work elsewhere. We have no conflicts of
interest to declare.

\closing{Sincerely,}
